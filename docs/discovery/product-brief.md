# Product Brief

## Problem

Profile-first dating encourages rapid appearance filtering and leaves two strangers with a blank one-to-one chat. The Pilot tests whether a scheduled group voice experience creates useful curiosity and conversational context before limited visual disclosure. This expected effect remains an assumption, not a validated outcome.

## Target user

Adults aged 25–39 who are 19+ on participation day, have dating intent, operate broadly in Seoul, and fit the mutually compatible heterosexual Pilot cohort policy. This is a reversible Pilot recruitment boundary, not a universal target-market claim.

## Approved value proposition

A private, scheduled six-person voice session uses three structured activities and free conversation before private interest, consent-gated limited reveal, final mutual selection, and optional pair voice.

## Approved session

- Exactly six admitted participants; target composition three women and three men.
- Ninety minutes: rules 5, three games 45, free conversation 20, initial interest 5, limited reveal 5, final selection and close 5, plus five minutes of operator-controlled recovery buffer within the stage timers.
- No participant browsing before assignment.
- Initial view: temporary nickname, five-year age band, Seoul activity area, and one interest clue.
- One required private face photo; reveal is never automatic and may be declined.
- Initial interest: up to two participants or none.
- Limited reveal: exact photo, exact age, and occupation category only after mutual initial interest and independent resource consent.
- Final romantic choice: one participant or none; compatible mutual choice grants a ten-minute pair voice room.

## Trust requirements

Account-bound reservation and admission, NICE-derived adult eligibility, short-lived RTC credentials, server-authoritative stages, no public counts or rejection reasons, deterministic contact/promotion controls, human-reviewable reporting and sanctions, and privacy-minimized retention.

## Exclusions

Payment/deposits, webcam, offline booking, private text before mutual progression, group recording, temporary subgroups, live facilitation, biometric/liveness/face comparison, raw identity documents, CI/DI, manual document review, relationship scoring, and popularity ranking.

## Success boundary

The Pilot may measure comprehension, safe completion, conversation continuation, pre/post-reveal interest changes, mutual voice progression, safe exit, reports, join quality, and willingness to return. Downloads, session length, revenue, laughter, or match count alone do not prove value or safety.

## Live-Pilot gates

Implementation documents are approved, but live participant operation remains blocked until NICE contract/legal review, LiveKit Korea device tests and cross-border review, NCP/Grafana procurement and privacy review, moderator readiness, retention notice review, and the PWA acceptance matrix pass.

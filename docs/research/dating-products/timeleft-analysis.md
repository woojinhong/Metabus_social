# Timeleft Analysis

## Document status

- **Classification:** research finding
- **Verified:** 2026-07-27
- **Current classification:** operating with substantial company-reported geographic and event expansion
- **Project authority:** external evidence only; no scheduled-group, friendship-first, venue, subscription, or facilitation decision is approved

## Current positioning

Timeleft calls itself a friendship and in-person connection service rather than a dating app. Adults answer a short questionnaire, book an event, receive an assigned small group and venue, and meet through a shared meal or activity.

## Target user and job

- **Confirmed fact:** adults seeking new friends and real-world social connection, including people new to a city or rebuilding social routines.
- **Research interpretation:** the product sells coordination and a shared social experience rather than profile access.
- **Boundary:** romance may occur, but official positioning does not make dating the primary purpose.

## Core interaction model

| Dimension | Current evidence |
| --- | --- |
| timing | scheduled; dinners are commonly weekly, with other event formats available |
| discovery | no ordinary profile browsing; Timeleft assigns a group |
| group | generally about six strangers, with some official pages describing 4–6 participants |
| evaluation | questionnaire, language, age, preferences and post-event feedback |
| conversation | shared meal/activity plus optional prompt game |
| location | public restaurant or event venue selected by the service |
| progression | post-event connection and messaging, but official sources conflict on whether this is automatic or requires mutual Likes |
| repeat | new groups, cities, restaurants and event types |

## Liquidity and cold start

Timeleft batches demand into a city, event type, and time. This reduces the need for always-on simultaneous presence but introduces:

- city-level participant density;
- group composition and language constraints;
- venue availability;
- cancellations, late cancellations and no-shows;
- replacement and support operations;
- repeat exposure to the same local pool.

The current help center says only available cities appear in signup and that cities are added regularly. This is direct evidence of city-by-city supply management.

## Conversation support

Participants share a meal or activity, and Timeleft provides prompts or a game. Everyone arrives under a common expectation to interact.

- **Confirmed mechanism:** shared context and prompt scaffolding are present.
- **Research interpretation:** this can reduce the blank-page problem.
- **Evidence gap:** no controlled evidence shows that the format is lower pressure, improves sustained conversation, or produces lasting friendship or romance for the relevant project segment.

## Trust, safety, and consent

Timeleft describes:

- public and selected venues;
- community guidelines;
- post-event feedback;
- reporting and blocking;
- future-match avoidance after blocking;
- a dedicated trust-and-safety function;
- AI-assisted review of reported messages;
- employee security and safety processes.

Public evidence reviewed does not establish universal identity verification, on-site facilitators at ordinary dinners, incident rates, response times, appeal processes, or offline-liability boundaries.

### Conflicting connection-flow evidence

- A current Help Center page says everyone from an event is connected automatically when it ends, after which a user can block to remove the connection.
- A current Korean App Store description says post-event chat follows mutual Likes.

**Evidence gap:** this may reflect region, version, or documentation drift. The research must not assert one universal consent flow.

## Retention and repeat use

Timeleft says feedback improves future grouping and that repeated attendance improves fit. It offers dinner, drinks, coffee, running and other formats. A subscription permits repeated booking during the billing period.

**Evidence gap:** independent repeat-attendance, cohort retention, successful-connection, no-show, and healthy-exit measures were not found.

## Monetization

Current official help material describes 1-, 3-, and 12-month auto-renewing subscription periods, with event access included and food or venue spend paid separately. Some app-store records also show tickets or region-specific purchases.

Current policy surfaces include:

- subscription renewal;
- separate app-store cancellation;
- generally non-refundable started billing periods;
- possible no-show fees;
- event cancellation windows;
- venue charges outside Timeleft.

These are not incidental details; they are part of the user trust and operating model.

## Current evidence of expansion and durability

Official pages conflict:

- the newsroom and careers pages report 200+ cities and 52 countries;
- the dinner page reports 300 cities and 60 countries;
- a 2026 blog post says more than 3 million guests;
- the dinner page publishes a monthly stranger-and-dinner volume claim, but the displayed values changed during same-day verification; no exact count is treated as stable evidence.

All are company-reported. The inconsistencies may reflect rapid expansion or stale content, but they prevent one precise scale statement. The safest classification is current operation with substantial self-reported expansion, not independently audited success.

## Known weaknesses and operating risks

- city and time-slot fill;
- venue quality and reservation failures;
- group-composition mismatch;
- no-shows and cancellation friction;
- auto-renewal and account/subscription separation;
- uncertain identity verification and on-site supervision;
- participant behavior after an event;
- scale claims and connection-flow documentation that conflict.

## Transferability limits

- Timeleft uses a centralized global operating and venue network.
- Scheduled batching can concentrate demand but requires enough initial city density.
- A shared meal can create context without guaranteeing comfort or safety.
- Subscriptions may encourage repeat attendance while also adding renewal and cancellation trust risks.
- Automatic connection and mutual-opt-in are materially different consent models; the public record does not resolve which is universal.

## Reusable research candidates

These are research candidates, not proposals:

- measure fill, cancellation, attendance and replacement separately;
- define explicit post-event consent and unwanted-rematch controls;
- test whether prompts or facilitation improve outcomes relative to selection effects;
- measure venue and facilitator quality;
- distinguish event satisfaction, connection, repeat use and healthy exit;
- test willingness to pay without implying purchase of romantic access.
